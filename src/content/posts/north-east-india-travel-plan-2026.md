## Overview

A single long bike trip covering three North-East states, structured with a work-from-home window in the middle.

**Starting point:** Kolkata

**States:** Assam → Meghalaya → Arunachal Pradesh

**Key destinations:**
- Shillong — the Scotland of the East
- Cherrapunji (Sohra) — wettest place on Earth, waterfalls, caves
- Dawki (Umngot River) — water so clear boats look like they float in air
- Mawlynnong — Asia's cleanest village
- Double Decker Living Root Bridge, Nongriat
- Tezpur — gateway to Arunachal, on the Brahmaputra
- Sela Pass — 4,170m, one of the highest motorable passes in the world
- Tawang — largest monastery in India, 1962 war memorials
- Kaziranga National Park — UNESCO site, highest density of one-horned rhinos
- Majuli — world's largest river island
- Anini — one of the most remote district headquarters in India, Dibang Valley, Idu Mishmi tribe

**Total trip:** ~28 days
**WFH window:** ~5 days (Days 10–12, flexible)

**Leave structure:**
| Block | Duration | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | ~7 days (incl. weekends) | Office leave + weekends |
| Phase 2 | ~5 days | Work from home (Dawki) |
| Phase 3 | ~15 days (incl. weekends) | Office leave + weekends |

> Numbers are approximate and will shift based on actual dates, permits, and road conditions.

## Permits & Documents

| Permit | For | Where to get |
|---|---|---|
| Inner Line Permit (ILP) | Arunachal Pradesh entry | Online: arunachalilp.in, or at checkposts in Bhalukpong / Balipara |
| Special Permit | Bum La Pass (India-China border) | Deputy Commissioner's office, Tawang town (takes 1 day) |
| No permit needed | Assam, Meghalaya | — |

**Carry at all times:**
- Valid driving licence (original + photocopy)
- Original vehicle RC book + insurance
- PUC certificate
- Aadhar / Voter ID (original) — ILP requires this
- 2 passport photos for ILP
- Bike papers (no NOC needed for solo intra-India travel, but carry the RC)

## Best Time to Go

**October to February** — dry weather, clear skies, roads mostly open, waterfalls still flowing from monsoon, Sela Pass accessible.

- **November** is the sweet spot: post-monsoon greenery still fresh, Sela Pass open, Kaziranga safaris running, Anini road conditions stable.
- **Avoid:** June–September (monsoon). Meghalaya roads turn dangerous, Sela Pass closes frequently, Anini road washes out.
- **Tawang note:** Sela Tunnel (opened 2024) provides all-weather connectivity so the pass no longer blocks Tawang in winter. But the high pass road (going up to 4170m) is still the scenic route — do it on the way in, use the tunnel on the way out.

## Phase 1 — Travel: Kolkata → Meghalaya

### Day 1 — Kolkata → Siliguri

**Distance:** ~570 km | **Road:** NH12 (old NH31) | **Estimated riding time:** 9–10 hours

- **Morning (6:00 AM):** Depart Kolkata. Exit via Kona Expressway / NH12. Fill fuel at Dankuni before the highway.
- **Mid-morning (8:30 AM):** Cross Bardhaman. Road is smooth four-lane here. Quick tea and paratha stop at any highway dhaba.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** Dalkhola / Raiganj area. Good dhabas for lunch — try dal-rice with mustard fish if you can find it locally. Fill fuel.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Cross into North Bengal. Road improves further. Pass through Islampur, Dalkhola stretch.
- **Late afternoon (4:30 PM):** Enter Siliguri. The Mahananda Wildlife Sanctuary flanks the road just before the city.
- **Evening (5:30 PM):** Check in. Siliguri has plenty of hotel options near Sevoke Road or Hill Cart Road — Hotel Chancellor, Hotel Sinclairs, or budget guesthouses.
- **Dinner:** Siliguri's Hill Cart Road has everything from momos to Bengali fish curry.

> **Fuel note:** Fill up at every major town. Long stretches without pumps start after this point.

---

### Day 2 — Siliguri → Guwahati

**Distance:** ~430 km | **Road:** NH27 (East–West Corridor) | **Estimated riding time:** 7–8 hours

- **Morning (6:00 AM):** Early start. NH27 through the Dooars is one of the most scenic stretches in the country — tea gardens on both sides, forested hills, and occasional elephant crossings.
- **Mid-morning (8:30 AM):** Cooch Behar — stop for tea. The Cooch Behar Palace is worth a 30-min detour if interested.
- **Midday (11:30 AM):** Cross into Assam at Golakganj / Dhubri. Enter a different world — flat Brahmaputra plains, wetlands, and the widening river.
- **Afternoon (1:00 PM):** Barpeta Road area — lunch stop. Assamese thali (rice, dal, roasted mustard fish, fried brinjal) at a local hotel.
- **Afternoon (3:30 PM):** Bongaigaon. Good fuel stop.
- **Evening (5:30 PM):** Arrive Guwahati. The city sits on the south bank of the Brahmaputra — the river is enormous here.
- **Check-in:** Hotel near Paltan Bazaar or Fancy Bazar area. Dynasty Hotel, Hotel Nandan, or OYO options near Kamakhya Road.
- **Evening:** Walk along the Brahmaputra riverfront (Uzan Bazar ghat). Watch sunset over the river — it is wide enough that the north bank disappears in haze.

---

### Day 3 — Guwahati explore

**Full day in city — no riding**

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Kamakhya Temple. One of the 51 Shakti Peethas, perched on Nilachal Hill. Go early to avoid queue. The temple architecture and the atmosphere inside the courtyard are intense. Allow 2 hours.
- **Mid-morning (10:00 AM):** Umananda Island — take a short ferry from Kachari Ghat to the small island in the middle of the Brahmaputra. Has a Shiva temple and golden langur monkeys. 1 hour round trip.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** Head to Pan Bazaar area. Lunch at Paradise Restaurant (Guwahati institution) or Anand Bhavan for local Assamese food.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Assam State Museum — covers NE India's archaeology, tribal culture, and sculpture. Good 1.5-hour visit.
- **Afternoon (4:00 PM):** Guwahati Zoo or Pobitora Wildlife Sanctuary (50 km east of Guwahati) — Pobitora has the highest density of one-horned rhinos in the world in a small area. Worth the detour if not going to Kaziranga on a tight schedule.
- **Evening (6:00 PM):** Brahmaputra cruise from Fancy Bazar ghat. An hour on the river at dusk.
- **Dinner:** Gam's Restaurant (Assamese) or Pan Bazaar area street food — lakhi pitha, khar fish curry.

> **ILP:** Apply online at arunachalilp.in today. Takes 1–2 days to process. You'll need it from Day 15 onwards.

---

### Day 4 — Guwahati → Shillong

**Distance:** ~100 km | **Road:** NH6 | **Estimated riding time:** 3–4 hours

- **Morning (8:00 AM):** Depart Guwahati. NH6 starts flat and suburban, then the landscape changes completely as you leave Assam.
- **Mid-morning (9:30 AM):** Cross the Assam-Meghalaya border at Jorabat. The air gets cooler and the road begins climbing into the Khasi Hills.
- **Mid-morning (10:00 AM):** **Umiam Lake (Barapani)** — 15 km before Shillong. A massive reservoir surrounded by pine-forested hills. Stop here for 30 minutes. The viewpoint above the lake gives a panoramic view of still blue water in the hills. Water sports available (kayaking, boating) if time permits.
- **Midday (11:00 AM):** Continue to Shillong. The city descends on you through pine-lined roads and suddenly you're in the heart of it.
- **Afternoon (12:30 PM):** Check in. Hotels near Police Bazaar: Hotel Polo Towers (solid mid-range), Hotel Centre Point, or boutique homestays in Laitumkhrah area.
- **Afternoon (2:30 PM):** Police Bazaar — the commercial heart of Shillong. Explore on foot. Small shops selling local handicrafts, bamboo products, Khasi shawls, smoked meat. Grab a Shillong plate (local street snack — jadoh rice with pork or chicken).
- **Evening (5:00 PM):** Ward's Lake — a small artificial lake inside a garden in the city centre. Peaceful evening walk. Entry ₹20. Boating available.
- **Evening (7:00 PM):** Café culture. Shillong is known for its music and café scene. Try Café Shillong, Dylan's Café (named after Bob Dylan, live music sometimes), or ML05 Café in Laitumkhrah.
- **Dinner:** Cloud 9 Restaurant or Delhi Darbar for varied options, or find local Khasi pork stew (dohneiiong) at smaller joints.

---

### Day 5 — Shillong explore

**Full day — no long riding**

- **Morning (6:30 AM):** Shillong Peak — the highest point in Shillong (1,965m). The approach road is narrow and steep — ideal for a bike. Indian Air Force controls the summit, but there is a civilian viewpoint just below. Clear mornings give views of Bangladesh plains below the escarpment. Allow 1.5 hours.
- **Morning (9:00 AM):** Elephant Falls — 3-tier waterfall, 12 km from the city. The first tier is broad and flat, the second dramatic, the third the most photographed. Easy descent down well-made steps. Entry ₹20.
- **Midday (11:00 AM):** Don Bosco Museum — seven floors covering the cultures, art, history, and ecology of all the North-East states. Best museum in the NE. Allow 2 hours. Entry ₹100.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Lunch near Police Bazaar. Jadoh (Khasi rice with pork blood and meat) from a local spot, or try smoked pork with sticky rice.
- **Afternoon (4:00 PM):** **Laitlum Canyon** — 24 km from Shillong, a dramatic canyon that translates to "End of the Hills." Deep V-shaped valleys, cloud-wrapped ridges, and small villages clinging to slopes. Bike ride to the viewpoint is the full experience. Sunset here is exceptional.
- **Evening (7:00 PM):** Return to Shillong. Live music venues come alive — check if anyone is playing at Cloud 9 or any bar on Police Bazaar area.

> **Optional:** Mawphlang Sacred Forest (25 km from Shillong) — a forest protected by Khasi customary law for centuries. Nothing can be removed or harmed. Ancient megaliths, rare plants. Go with a local guide (mandatory). Can swap with Laitlum if needed.

### Day 6 — Shillong → Cherrapunji (Sohra)

**Distance:** ~55 km | **Road:** winding hill road via Mawkdok | **Estimated riding time:** 2 hours

- **Morning (8:00 AM):** Depart Shillong. The road immediately narrows and starts winding through pine forest.
- **Mid-morning (9:00 AM):** **Mawkdok Dympep Valley Viewpoint** — roughly halfway on the Shillong–Sohra road. The valley drops away below into a deep gorge. On a clear morning the clouds are below you. Stop for 20 minutes.
- **Morning (10:00 AM):** Arrive at Sohra (Cherrapunji). This plateau edges the great escarpment — the land drops 1,200m in a few kilometers straight down to the Bangladesh plains. On clear days, you see Bangladesh.
- **Morning (10:30 AM):** Check in at a guesthouse or eco-resort. Cherrapunjee Holiday Resort is good and overlooks the gorge. Polo Orchid Resort. Budget: NG Eco Homestay, Cascade Homestay.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** **Nohkalikai Falls** — the tallest plunge waterfall in India, 340m drop. A short walk from the viewpoint to the edge of the cliff. The pool at the bottom is a strange turquoise green. In November, water volume is still substantial from the monsoon. The story behind the name is tragic — look it up.
- **Afternoon (1:30 PM):** **Seven Sisters Falls (Nohsngithiang Falls)** — just 4 km from Nohkalikai. Seven separate streams cascade down the same limestone cliff face simultaneously. Best during and just after monsoon (Aug–Oct). In November still partially flowing. Wide viewpoint across the gorge. Entry ₹20.
- **Afternoon (3:00 PM):** **Eco Park** — a government park on the cliff edge overlooking Bangladesh. Sunset-facing viewpoint with multiple waterfalls visible in the gorge below.
- **Afternoon (4:30 PM):** **Mawsmai Cave** — 150m lit limestone cave, stalactites, stalagmites, narrow passages. The easiest cave in Meghalaya. Entry ₹20.
- **Evening (6:00 PM):** Return to guesthouse. The evening mist rolls in fast off the escarpment. Sit with tea and watch it.
- **Dinner:** Orange Roots restaurant in Sohra (known for local Khasi food), or Jiva Grill.

---

### Day 7 — Cherrapunji waterfalls & caves

**Base: Cherrapunji. No major riding.**

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** **Wei Sawdong Falls** — a hidden three-tier waterfall, 5 km from Sohra. Takes a 30-min trek down through forest. The three tiers step down through jungle, the bottom pool is aquamarine blue and you can swim. Fewer visitors than other falls. Start early.
- **Mid-morning (10:00 AM):** Back to Sohra. Breakfast.
- **Morning (11:00 AM):** **Arwah Cave** — fossil-bearing limestone cave, 4 km from Mawsmai. The walls embed ancient marine fossils 65–70 million years old. Different from Mawsmai — drier, longer passages. Entry ₹30.
- **Midday (1:00 PM):** Lunch at guesthouse.
- **Afternoon (2:30 PM):** **Garden of Caves** — a small park near Sohra with caves, waterfalls, and walking trails through the gorge. Entry ₹20. Good for 1.5 hours of easy exploration.
- **Afternoon (4:00 PM):** **Dainthlen Falls** — associated with a Khasi serpent legend. The waterfall flows through a large rocky gorge. Short walk from the road. The scale of the gorge is impressive.
- **Evening (6:00 PM):** Back at guesthouse. Plan tomorrow's root bridge trek. Carry: water (2L), snacks, good shoes, fully charged phone, and download the offline map for Tyrna–Nongriat trail.

## Phase 2 — WFH: Meghalaya Base

### Day 8 — Double Decker Root Bridge Trek

**Full day off — most physically demanding day of the trip**

- **Morning (6:00 AM):** Early start, drive 20 km from Cherrapunji to Tyrna village. The last section down to the trailhead is steep and rough — park the bike safely at the village parking.
- **Morning (6:30 AM):** Begin descent. **3,500 stone steps** going down into the valley. Dense jungle, suspension bridges over streams, and increasingly humid air as you descend.
- **Mid-morning (9:00 AM):** Pass the **Single Root Bridge** (first living root bridge en route). Rubber fig tree roots trained across a stream over generations, now fully load-bearing. Surreal.
- **Morning (10:30 AM):** Reach **Nongriat village**. Small settlement, a few homestays. Entry ₹50.
- **Morning (10:45 AM):** **Double Decker Living Root Bridge** — two bridges stacked above each other, the upper one more than 150 years old, the lower one around 80. Both alive, growing, maintained by the same Khasi family for generations. Walk across slowly. The roots are worn smooth and are completely reliable.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** Natural pools near Nongriat. Turquoise freshwater pools formed in the rocky riverbed. Swim. Rest. This is the payoff.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Begin the climb back. 3,500 steps going up is a serious physical effort. Take breaks every 500 steps.
- **Afternoon (4:00 PM):** Reach Tyrna. Drink something with sugar immediately.
- **Evening (5:30 PM):** Back at Cherrapunji guesthouse. Hot shower, food, early sleep.

> **Note:** If you want to avoid the brutal return climb, you can stay overnight at Nongriat village in a homestay (basic but functional) and climb back in the morning when it is cooler.

### Day 9 — Cherrapunji → Mawlynnong → Dawki

**Distance:** ~85 km | **Road:** Cherrapunji → Pynursla → Mawlynnong → Dawki | **Riding time:** 4–5 hours

- **Morning (8:00 AM):** Depart Cherrapunji. Head towards Pynursla — the road descends from the plateau into lower hills.
- **Morning (9:30 AM):** Pynursla — small town, fuel stop.
- **Mid-morning (10:30 AM):** **Mawlynnong Village** — Asia's cleanest village, awarded 2003. 90 km from Shillong. The community maintains every path, garden, and drainage with bamboo dustbins and collective effort. No litter, no open drains. Walk through the village (entry ₹20), visit the **Riwai Living Root Bridge** (5-min walk from the village, a single-span bridge — easier than Nongriat but still impressive), and climb the **bamboo skywalk** platform in the canopy for views into Bangladesh on a clear day.
- **Afternoon (1:00 PM):** Lunch in Mawlynnong. Small local eateries serve rice and local vegetable dishes.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Continue to **Dawki** (Shnongpdeng) — ~20 km. The road descends sharply. On the final approach you see the river through the trees — and it looks unreal. The **Umngot River** water is so transparent that boats hover above a visible riverbed. This is not a photographic trick.
- **Afternoon (3:00 PM):** Check in at Shnongpdeng campsites or riverside guesthouses. This will be the WFH base for the next few days.
- **Afternoon (4:30 PM):** **Boat on the Umngot River** — take a wooden coracle on the river. ₹800–₹1,500 per boat. The transparency is completely disorienting in person.
- **Evening:** Sit at the riverside. The hills of Bangladesh are directly across the water. Campfire if the campsite arranges one.

### Days 10–12 — WFH from Dawki/Shnongpdeng

**Working remotely from the river. Network: Jio/Airtel 4G functional here.**

Each day follows roughly this rhythm. Days vary based on workload.

**Workday structure:**
- 7:00 AM: Morning walk along the riverbank.
- 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM: WFH. Most homestays and campsites have seating areas. Carry a portable WiFi/hotspot as a backup.
- After work:

**Day 10 evening:** **Cliff jumping** at Shnongpdeng. There are specific rocky ledges that locals and guides point out — 10–15 foot jumps into deep clear water. Guide: ~₹300.

**Day 11 — Day trip:** **Krang Suri Falls** (80 km from Dawki, takes 1.5 hrs each way). If you can take a half-day or full day off — this is the most visually stunning waterfall in Meghalaya. The water is an impossible turquoise blue. A natural pool beneath the falls. Go on a weekday morning for fewer crowds. Entry ₹50.

**Day 12 evening:** Explore **Dawki border crossing** — the India-Bangladesh crossing at Tamabil is 2 km from Shnongpdeng. You cannot cross, but you can stand at the gate and watch the international trade trucks roll in and out.

> **Connectivity note:** Keep a Jio SIM as primary and Airtel as backup. BSNL is the last fallback in deep NE. Download all work files offline before Anini.

## Phase 3 — Travel: Tawang, Kaziranga & Anini

### Day 13 — Dawki → Guwahati

**Distance:** ~180 km | **Road:** Dawki → Amlarem → Jowai → Guwahati | **Riding time:** 5–6 hours

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Depart Dawki. The return route goes via Amlarem rather than Shillong — new roads, forested hills, very little traffic.
- **Mid-morning (9:00 AM):** **Phe Phe Falls** detour (if time permits) — a hidden three-tier falls near Amlarem, accessible via a short jungle walk. Not well signposted; ask locals. Worth it if you missed it earlier.
- **Midday (11:00 AM):** **Jowai** — headquarters of West Jaintia Hills. The Jaintia tribal culture is distinct from the Khasi. Jowai sits above a river gorge. 30-min exploration.
- **Afternoon (2:30 PM):** Arrive Guwahati.
- **Afternoon (3:30 PM):** Check in. Stock up: cash (ATMs will get sparse), medicines, rain gear.
- **Evening:** Rest and plan. Confirm ILP status (should have arrived by now). Print a copy if possible.

---

### Day 14 — Guwahati → Tezpur

**Distance:** ~180 km | **Road:** NH15 / NH13 | **Riding time:** 3.5–4 hours

- **Morning (8:00 AM):** Depart Guwahati north on NH15 (Trans-Arunachal section). Flat plains of Assam, occasional tea garden stretches. Fast road.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** Arrive **Tezpur** — gateway to western Arunachal Pradesh, on the south bank of the Brahmaputra. The Brahmaputra is at its widest and wildest here.
- **Afternoon (1:00 PM):** Lunch in Tezpur town.
- **Afternoon (2:30 PM):** **Agnigarh Hill** — a hilltop above Tezpur, associated with the legend of Usha and Aniruddha (from the Mahabharata). There's a viewpoint with views over the Brahmaputra plains. 30 min.
- **Afternoon (3:30 PM):** **Da Parbatia ruins** — a 5th-century Gupta-period temple doorway carved from stone and left standing alone in a field. One of the oldest pieces of sculptural art in Assam. Quiet and undervisited.
- **Evening (5:30 PM):** **Nameri National Park** vicinity (15 km from Tezpur) — a buffer zone entry is possible for birdwatching at dusk. The Jia Bhoroli river runs through.
- **Evening:** Check in at Hotel Luit or Hotel Sripuria in Tezpur. Good options for the price.

---

### Day 15 — Tezpur → Dirang (via Bhalukpong & Bomdila)

**Distance:** ~200 km | **Road:** NH13 | **Riding time:** 6–7 hours | **Arunachal entry today**

- **Morning (6:30 AM):** Depart Tezpur early. The plains end abruptly and the foothills rise.
- **Morning (8:30 AM):** **Bhalukpong** — the ILP checkpost. Present your Inner Line Permit here. Police register your bike number and details. Take 20–30 minutes. The Kameng River (clear, fast-flowing) flows alongside the road here.
- **Mid-morning (9:00 AM):** Enter Arunachal Pradesh. The road climbs aggressively from here. NH13 is an engineering marvel cut into the hillside.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** **Bomdila** (2,415m) — a small hill town with a very pleasant climate. Three Buddhist monasteries (Upper, Middle, Lower Gontse Gang). The middle monastery has a large Buddha statue with sweeping views. Take 1–1.5 hours here.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Lunch in Bomdila market. Try Assamese-Tibetan fusion — momo soup (thukpa), fried rice, pork preparations.
- **Afternoon (3:30 PM):** Continue to **Dirang** (1,450m). The road descends briefly into the Dirang Chu valley — a beautiful river valley with terraced farms.
- **Afternoon (5:00 PM):** Arrive Dirang. Check in at Hotel Pemaling, Hotel Pinewood, or a guesthouse.
- **Afternoon (5:30 PM):** **Dirang Hot Spring** — a natural hot-water spring 2 km from town. Small facility, but the water is sulphurous and genuinely hot. Good for muscles after riding.
- **Evening:** Walk to **Dirang Dzong** — an old stone fort village above the town. The compact cluster of traditional stone houses is like a medieval settlement.
- **Dinner:** Hotel restaurant. Try local Monpa dishes — corn-based food, dried yak meat if available.

> **Fuel:** Fill at Bomdila. The next reliable pump is Tawang itself.

---

### Day 16 — Dirang → Sela Pass → Tawang

**Distance:** ~140 km | **Road:** NH13, including Sela Pass (4,170m) | **Riding time:** 5–6 hours | **Most dramatic riding day**

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Depart Dirang. Start early — the pass road is better in morning light before afternoon clouds roll in.
- **Morning (7:30 AM):** **Sangti Valley** — a flat, open valley 12 km from Dirang, known for black-necked cranes that winter here (Oct–March). A short diversion through open meadows with Himalayan peaks rising above.
- **Morning (9:00 AM):** The road starts the serious climb toward Sela. Every hairpin reveals a higher view of the valley you are leaving behind. Temperature drops.
- **Mid-morning (10:30 AM):** **Sela Pass** — 4,170m. The summit is marked by prayer flags and a small CRPF/Army post. At this altitude the air is thin, the light is sharp and blue. **Sela Lake** is immediately below the road — a large, often partially frozen lake with no vegetation, surrounded by brown-yellow tundra. In November it may have ice on the edges. Stop here — this is one of the highest points you will reach on the bike.
  - The pass was named after **Sela**, a Monpa girl who helped Indian soldier Jaswant Singh Rawat hold off Chinese forces for 72 hours alone during the 1962 war.
  - The **Sela Tunnel** (opened March 2024) provides an alternative route 400m below the pass — take the tunnel on your return trip. Going up, take the old pass road for the full altitude experience.
- **Mid-morning (11:30 AM):** Descend from Sela. The landscape changes as you drop — from alpine tundra to rhododendron forest to temperate broadleaf.
- **Midday (12:30 PM):** **Jaswant Garh War Memorial** (just before Jang town) — a memorial to Jaswant Singh Rawat, the soldier from the 1962 war. The army maintains his room exactly as it was when he died, treating him as still on active duty. Haunting and worth the stop.
- **Afternoon (1:00 PM):** **Jang town** — lunch. Basic restaurants, momo shops.
- **Afternoon (1:30 PM):** **Nuranang Falls (Jang Falls)** — 2 km east of Jang. Named after **Noora**, the other Monpa girl from the same 1962 story. A 100m-wide curtain of white water dropping into a rocky basin. Visible from the road.
- **Afternoon (3:00 PM):** Arrive **Tawang** (2,669m). The town opens up dramatically on a mountain shoulder. Check in. Hotel Tawang Heights, Hotel Sangay (has great valley views), or the government Tourism Lodge.
- **Evening (5:00 PM):** Walk through Tawang market. Small shops selling Tibetan handicrafts, thangka paintings, prayer wheels, yak wool blankets, local honey. Warm woollen sweaters from Monpa weavers.
- **Dinner:** Momo, thukpa, fried rice. Tawang has several small restaurants around the market. Try any local kitchen.

---

### Day 17 — Tawang: monastery, war memorial, town

**Full day in Tawang**

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** **Tawang Monastery (Galden Namgey Lhatse)** — founded 1681, largest Buddhist monastery in India, second largest in the world after Potala Palace in Lhasa. Five-storey structure, housing 500 monks. The main hall has a 8m gold-painted Buddha statue. The monastery library holds 400-year-old manuscripts. The architecture looks directly up at glacial peaks.
  - The **sixth Dalai Lama** was born in Tawang. When the current (14th) Dalai Lama escaped Tibet in 1959, he entered India through Tawang.
  - Allow 2–3 hours. Go early for fewer visitors and better light.
- **Mid-morning (10:00 AM):** **Urgelling Monastery** — smaller monastery near the main one, at the exact birthplace of the sixth Dalai Lama. Quiet, older structure.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** **Tawang War Memorial** — a large memorial in the town, commemorating 2,420 Indian soldiers who died in the 1962 Sino-Indian War. The inscribed names on the walls are sobering. Flame, sound and light show in the evening.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Lunch in Tawang market.
- **Afternoon (3:30 PM):** **PT Tso Lake (Pankang Teng Tso)** — 17 km from Tawang, a high-altitude lake at ~4,000m. Less visited than Madhuri Lake. The road goes through army areas — carry your ILP.
- **Evening (6:00 PM):** Return to town. Sound and Light show at the war memorial at dusk.
- **Dinner:** Tibetan Kitchen restaurant or hotel restaurant. Try Zan (Monpa staple food — buckwheat paste) if any restaurant offers it.

> **Apply today** for the Bum La Pass special permit at the Deputy Commissioner's office in Tawang town (₹100, takes 1 day). Carry 2 photos and your ILP.

---

### Day 18 — Tawang: Madhuri Lake & Bum La Pass

**Two different day trips — choose based on permit status and energy**

**Option A: Bum La Pass (if permit received)**

- **Morning (6:00 AM):** Depart with a registered guide (mandatory for Bum La). 40 km from Tawang, mostly on rough military road.
- **Morning (8:30 AM):** **Bum La Pass (4,793m)** — the India-China border at the top of the world. Standing at the actual Line of Actual Control. Chinese bunkers visible 200m away. Indian Army jawans at the post. This is a surreal experience — standing at a frozen alpine border between two nuclear powers.
  - The pass is only open to Indian citizens. No foreign nationals.
  - The Indian Army operates the point and is generally welcoming to civilians with permits.
- **Midday (1:00 PM):** Return to Tawang for lunch.
- **Afternoon (3:00 PM):** **Madhuri Lake (Shungatser Lake)** — 35 km from Tawang, 4,000m altitude. Also called Madhuri Lake because a Bollywood song was shot here. A blue-green lake ringed by snow peaks and yak grazing meadows. The approach road passes through stunning open highland terrain.

**Option B: Madhuri Lake + valley exploration (if no Bum La permit)**

- **Morning (8:00 AM):** Ride to Madhuri Lake. 35 km, ~1.5 hours on mountain road.
- **Morning (10:00 AM):** At the lake. Time, silence, snow peaks, yaks. One of the most beautiful spots on this entire trip.
- **Midday (12:30 PM):** Return toward Tawang. Detour through **Zemithang valley** (80 km from Tawang) if adventurous — a remote Monpa valley near the Bhutan border with traditional villages rarely visited.
- **Afternoon (3:00 PM):** Back in Tawang. Rest.

- **Evening:** Final evening in Tawang. Buy Monpa handicrafts, local honey, prayer flags for back home.

---

### Day 19 — Tawang → Bomdila (return)

**Distance:** ~165 km | **Road:** NH13 (return via Sela Tunnel) | **Riding time:** 5–6 hours

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Depart Tawang. Morning light over the monastery valley.
- **Morning (9:30 AM):** Approach the Sela Tunnel instead of the old pass road on the return. The **Sela Tunnel** (world's longest bi-lane tunnel at 13,000 feet, 3,000m altitude) was inaugurated in March 2024. Going through it is a different experience — cool, dark, echoey — and cuts 10 km off the Dirang–Tawang distance. The tunnel guarantees all-weather connectivity.
- **Midday (12:30 PM):** Dirang. Quick tea stop.
- **Afternoon (2:30 PM):** Bomdila. Check in. Light afternoon.
- **Evening:** Visit **Bomdila Monastery** (Lower Gontse Gang Monastery) — best one of the three, with an excellent view of the Kameng valley and Himalayan foothills stretching south. Apple orchards line the approach.

---

### Day 20 — Bomdila → Guwahati → Kaziranga

**Distance:** ~360 km total | **Riding time:** 7–8 hours**

- **Morning (6:00 AM):** Early start from Bomdila. Descend back through Bhalukpong and out of Arunachal.
- **Morning (9:00 AM):** Exit Arunachal at Bhalukpong. Back into Assam's flat plains.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** Pass through Tezpur. Skip Guwahati today — go directly east on NH27.
- **Afternoon (3:00 PM):** Arrive **Kaziranga** (Kohora town — the main tourist gate area). Check in. Iora Resort, Infinity Resort Kaziranga, or budget options near Kohora.
- **Afternoon (4:00 PM):** Book morning safari at the resort front desk. **Central Range jeep safari** is the most popular. Need to book ahead in season.
- **Evening:** Walk along the road near the park boundary. Elephants sometimes cross at dusk near the park gates.

---

### Day 21 — Kaziranga National Park

**Full day — two safaris**

- **Morning (5:30 AM):** **Morning jeep safari — Central Range (Kohora)** — the main zone for rhino sightings. Open grasslands, jhils (lakes), and tall elephant grass. The one-horned Indian rhinoceros is enormous at close range. You will also see: wild water buffalo (largest in the world, genuinely intimidating), swamp deer, Indian elephants, fishing cats, various waterbirds. Possible tiger sighting.
  - Safaris last ~3 hours.
  - Entry fee (Indian): ~₹250 + vehicle charges.
- **Morning (9:00 AM):** Return. Breakfast.
- **Morning (10:30 AM):** **Eastern Range (Agaratoli)** — more remote, fewer tourists. Better chance of seeing rhinos without crowds. Elephants frequent this zone.
- **Afternoon (1:00 PM):** Lunch. Rest during the midday heat.
- **Afternoon (3:30 PM):** **Western Range (Bagori)** — open grassy area with good visibility, known for large buffalo herds. Sunset safari — golden light over the grasslands.
- **Evening:** Kaziranga is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with the highest density of one-horned rhinos in the world (~2,400 animals in ~430 km²). Sit with that scale.

> **Note:** Kaziranga is closed during monsoon (roughly June–October). If visiting in November, opening day can vary — confirm in advance. Best season is November–April.

---

### Day 22 — Kaziranga → Majuli Island

**Distance:** ~95 km to Jorhat + ferry to Majuli | **Road:** NH27 to Jorhat | **Riding time:** 2 hours

- **Morning (8:00 AM):** Depart Kaziranga east on NH27.
- **Morning (10:00 AM):** Arrive **Jorhat** (100 km). Tea capital of Assam — the oldest tea auction centre. If interested, visit a tea estate on the way.
- **Morning (11:00 AM):** Head to **Nimatighat**, 17 km from Jorhat. The ferry ghat for Majuli Island.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** Board the ferry to **Majuli** — the world's largest river island (880 km²), sitting in the middle of the Brahmaputra. The ferry takes 1–1.5 hours. You ride your bike onto the flat-bottomed ferry along with trucks, motorcycles, and livestock. The scale of the Brahmaputra from the water is staggering.
- **Afternoon (1:30 PM):** Land on Majuli. Check in at a guesthouse in Garamur or Kamalabari area — both have simple but clean options.
- **Afternoon (2:30 PM):** **Kamalabari Satra** — one of Majuli's famous Vaishnavite monasteries (satras). Founded in the 1500s. Monks practice traditional Sattriya dance and perform nightly prayers. The monastery compound, the wooden prayer hall, and the ancient masked dance props are all accessible.
- **Evening (5:00 PM):** Cycle through villages (rent a cycle from the guesthouse). Majuli has no major roads — it is flat, green, and unhurried. Water everywhere, egrets in the rice fields, fishermen with traditional Mising boats.
- **Evening:** Attend the evening prayer at any satra — drumming, cymbals, call-and-response singing. Deeply meditative.

---

### Day 23 — Majuli → Jorhat → Dibrugarh

**Distance (Jorhat → Dibrugarh):** ~55 km | **Road:** NH715 | **Riding time:** 1.5 hours

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Early morning on Majuli. Sunrise over the Brahmaputra from any riverbank — mist on the water, fishing boats, bird activity.
- **Morning (9:00 AM):** Visit **Auniati Satra** — the largest and most famous satra on Majuli. Holds a museum of Ahom-era artefacts, royal jewellery, and ancient manuscripts.
- **Midday (11:00 AM):** Head to Nimatighat. Board the noon ferry back to the mainland.
- **Afternoon (1:00 PM):** Off the ferry at Nimatighat. Continue east toward Dibrugarh.
- **Afternoon (3:00 PM):** Arrive **Dibrugarh** — the oil and tea capital of Upper Assam. Situated on the Brahmaputra. This is your staging base for the Anini approach.
- **Afternoon (4:00 PM):** Check in. Hotel Gateway (reliable), Hotel Aroma, or similar.
- **Afternoon (5:00 PM):** Fuel up the bike completely. Spare fuel in a 5L jerry can if possible — the last reliable pump before Anini may be at Roing (still 165 km away), and Anini itself has only a government pump of uncertain availability.
- **Evening:** Get offline maps downloaded for Roing–Anini stretch. This route has essentially no mobile network from Hunli onwards.
- **Check supplies:** Food for 2 days (biscuits, dry fruits, instant noodles, energy bars), water purification tablets, first aid kit, bike tools, spare tube.

---

### Day 24 — Dibrugarh → Roing

**Distance:** ~165 km | **Road:** NH315/NH13 via Bogibeel Bridge or Brahmaputra ferry | **Riding time:** 4–5 hours

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Depart Dibrugarh. Cross the Brahmaputra at the **Bogibeel Bridge** (India's longest rail-road bridge, 4.94 km), opened 2018. The bridge itself is a remarkable piece of infrastructure in an area that was previously ferry-dependent.
- **Morning (9:00 AM):** Enter Lower Dibang Valley district after crossing. The road follows the north bank of the Brahmaputra.
- **Midday (12:00 PM):** Arrive **Roing** — headquarters of Lower Dibang Valley district. This is the last major town before Anini. The town has markets, ATMs, a government hospital, and reasonably reliable mobile network.
- **Afternoon (1:00 PM):** Lunch in Roing. Fill up fuel completely.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** **Mehao Lake and Wildlife Sanctuary** — a beautiful lake 25 km from Roing, accessible by vehicle. Mehao Wildlife Sanctuary has gayal (mithun), tigers, and rare birds. Good for an afternoon exploration.
- **Afternoon (4:30 PM):** Return to Roing. Check in. Government Circuit House or a local guesthouse.
- **Evening:** Speak to locals or the Circuit House caretaker about current road conditions on the Roing–Anini road. The route frequently has landslides and washed-out sections. Get current information.
- Withdraw cash. There is no ATM in Anini.

---

### Day 25 — Roing → Anini

**Distance:** ~228 km | **Road:** extremely rough mountain road | **Riding time:** 8–14 hours (depending on condition) | **Hardest riding day of the trip**

> **This is the most challenging day of the entire journey.** The Roing–Anini road climbs through the Dibang Valley into territory that has no mobile network, no fuel, almost no habitation, and road conditions that change with every season. Do this only if your bike has decent ground clearance, you are an experienced off-road rider, and you have checked the current road status. A Royal Enfield Himalayan or similar adventure bike is strongly recommended.

- **Morning (5:30 AM):** Start before dawn. The goal is to reach Anini before dark. Do not attempt this road in the dark.
- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Pass through **Hunli** (~100 km from Roing) — last small settlement with any facilities. Possible basic food available. Fill any spare fuel containers here.
- **Morning onward:** The road follows the **Dibang River** (one of the most powerful rivers in India in terms of water volume). It is raw Himalayan mountain terrain — bamboo forest, rushing streams, sheer drops, and occasional army vehicles.
- **Midday:** Cross multiple rivers on bridge structures that vary in condition. Take each crossing carefully.
- **Afternoon (2:00–5:00 PM, depending on start):** Arrive **Anini** (1,968m) — headquarters of Dibang Valley district, population ~4,500. The town sits on a plateau between the Dri River and Mathun River, tributaries of the Dibang.
  - There is no town here in the conventional sense. Government buildings, a hospital, two schools, and the homes of the few hundred permanent residents — mostly government employees and Idu Mishmi tribal people.
  - The **Idu Mishmi** are one of the most isolated tribal communities in India. They have their own language (Midu dialect, only spoken in Anini), animist-Buddhist religious practices, and a culture almost entirely unchanged.
- **Evening:** Check in to the **Circuit House** (book ahead through the Dibang Valley District Administration if possible) or ask at the PWD guesthouse. These are the only accommodation options.
- **Dinner:** Cook yourself if you brought supplies, or inquire at the Circuit House — the caretaker often cooks for visitors.
- The **Dibang Wildlife Sanctuary** begins just north of town.

---

### Day 26 — Anini explore

**Rest day and exploration in Anini**

- **Morning (7:00 AM):** Walk down to the **Dri River** — the roar of the water is constant. The river is violent, glacier-fed, milky blue. Cane bridges cross it at certain points — the traditional suspension bridges made entirely from locally grown cane, some of which are centuries old and still functional.
- **Morning (9:00 AM):** Visit the **Idu Mishmi households** near the Circuit House. If you have a local contact or the Circuit House caretaker introduces you, this is an opportunity to see the interior of a traditional Mishmi home — woven bamboo construction, central hearth, hunting trophies.
- **Midday:** The town's daily rhythm is simple — government offices open, small market stalls, army presence in the background.
- **Afternoon (2:00 PM):** Walk toward the **Dibang Wildlife Sanctuary** boundary north of town. Red panda, Mishmi takin, musk deer, Asiatic black bear are present in the sanctuary. Birdlife is extraordinary — Sclater's monal (one of the rarest pheasants on earth), Blyth's tragopan. You will not see large animals on a casual walk, but the habitat and the silence are themselves the experience.
- **Evening:** Sit at the edge of the plateau. The Dibang valley disappears into mist and Himalayan ridges on all sides. Anini is surrounded by peaks rising to 5,000m. In clear weather, snow visible on the crests.
- This is roughly 1,800–2,000m above sea level, on a plateau that should not exist at the foot of the Himalayas. It is one of the least visited places in India for a reason — and that reason is exactly why it is worth coming to.

---

### Day 27 — Anini → Roing → Dibrugarh

**Distance:** ~390 km | **Road:** Anini → Roing (rough) + Roing → Dibrugarh | **Riding time:** 10–14 hours total

- **Morning (5:00 AM):** Start before dawn. The Anini–Roing road requires daylight throughout. Start immediately.
- **Morning (9:30–11:00 AM):** Reach Hunli. Tea and snacks.
- **Afternoon (2:00–4:00 PM):** Reach Roing. Fuel, food, and a moment of relief.
- **Afternoon (4:00 PM):** Decision point: stay in Roing (shorter day) or push on to Dibrugarh (better facilities).
- If pushing: ~3 more hours to Dibrugarh via Bogibeel Bridge.
- **Evening:** Arrive Dibrugarh. Hot shower. Proper food.

---

### Day 28 — Head home

**Options:**
- **Option A — Ride to Guwahati, take overnight train:** Dibrugarh to Guwahati is ~490 km (8–9 hours). Arrive Guwahati evening, take Kamrup Express or Saraighat Express overnight to Kolkata (arrives ~7 AM the next day). Bike goes in the guard van (book in advance). This is the most efficient end to the trip.
- **Option B — Ride all the way to Kolkata:** Dibrugarh → Kolkata is ~1,060 km (2 days). Dibrugarh → Siliguri (~480 km) on Day 28, Siliguri → Kolkata (~570 km) on Day 29.
- **Option C — Fly back from Dibrugarh:** Dibrugarh airport (Mohanbari Airport) has flights to Kolkata. Arrange bike transport separately if needed.

## Logistics & Tips

### Fuel Strategy
| Stretch | Situation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Kolkata → Siliguri | Pumps everywhere | No issue |
| Siliguri → Guwahati | Dooars, occasional gaps | Fill at every major town |
| Guwahati → Shillong | Easy | No issue |
| Shillong → Dawki | Cherrapunji and Dawki have pumps | Fill at Sohra before Dawki |
| Dawki → Tezpur | Fill at Jowai and Guwahati | No issue |
| Tezpur → Tawang | **Fill at Bomdila** | Next pump only at Tawang (160+ km away) |
| Kaziranga → Dibrugarh | Regular pumps | No issue |
| Dibrugarh → Anini | **Fill full at Roing** | No pump until you return |

> Carry 3–5L of spare fuel in a jerry can from Roing onward.

### Mobile Network
| Area | Network |
|---|---|
| Assam / Meghalaya (towns) | Jio 4G or Airtel 4G |
| Dawki / Shnongpdeng | Jio functional |
| Bomdila / Dirang | Jio / Airtel, spotty |
| Tawang | BSNL best, Jio/Airtel partial |
| Anini and Roing–Anini road | **Nothing from Hunli to Anini** |

> Download offline Google Maps (Arunachal Pradesh entire state) before Day 15. Keep a physical road atlas or printout as backup.

### Money
- Carry sufficient cash from Guwahati before the Arunachal stretch.
- Last ATMs: Roing (for Anini leg), Bomdila (for Tawang leg).
- Tawang has 1–2 ATMs but they are not always stocked.
- Anini: **no ATM**.
